Scene Activity

Cloud Archiving is the biggest lever, by far. Across 40 cameras of measured production traffic, cameras with archiving on uploaded a median 1047 kbps; cameras with it off uploaded 58 kbps — an 18× difference. Retention length (30 d vs 270 d) made no measurable difference, because it governs how long footage is stored, not how much is sent. Toggle it per model in the Archive column below.
Scene activity is the second lever: it sets what fraction of a camera's encoder ceiling actually goes out. A static server room realised 0.1% of its ceiling; a busy entrance realised 100%. FOV note — 200 people/hr across 4 cameras is ~50/hr per FOV, so rate each camera on its own view.

Provenance. Encoder ladders come from VIDEO_AGENT_LOG dash-quality transitions on one online unit of each of 17 models, ~21 h per model. Activity factors and peak ratios come from a 7-day hourly bandwidth export covering 41 cameras and 7 DR40 readers. Fifteen devices appear in both datasets, and on those the log-derived ceiling predicted actual bytes within ±17% wherever the scene was active.

Known limits. All measurements come from one organisation, so per-model sample sizes are small (1–5 cameras). AI feature overhead is not separately validated. Activity is the single largest source of error: two identical cameras in the same building differed by 500× purely on scene content, so rate each camera on its own view rather than applying one profile site-wide. Recommended ISP speed does not account for non-Rhombus traffic sharing the uplink.
